# Copy & Grammar Checklist (Spanish + English)

Check every line of transcribed copy. Album, artist, and track names follow Amazon Music styling (see amazon-music-styling.md), not general grammar rules, so don't "correct" stylized names.

## Spanish

- **Accents (tildes)**: álbum, canción, emoción, pulmón, música, también, más (more) vs mas, tú (you) vs tu (your), él vs el, sí vs si, qué/cómo/cuándo/dónde in questions and exclamations, aquí, día, corazón, mamá
- **Opening punctuation**: questions need ¿...? and exclamations need ¡...!
- **Ñ**: año, mañana, sueño (never "ano" for "año")
- **Agreement**: gender/number between articles, nouns, and adjectives (todas las emociones, el álbum); subject–verb agreement
- **Verb forms and tense**: consistent person across a series (e.g., all first-person present: Escucho / Empiezo / Canto / Llego)
- **Capitalization**: Spanish doesn't capitalize days, months, languages, or nationalities mid-sentence; titles in sentence case unless it's a stylized name
- **Common mistakes**: haber/a ver, hay/ahí/ay, echo/hecho, porque/por qué/porqué, a/ha, halla/haya, sino/si no
- **Regional tone**: flag as an error only slang that clearly won't work for the target market (e.g., Mexico-only or Argentina-only words, voseo, in a pan-regional asset)

## English

- Spelling, homophones (its/it's, your/you're, their/there)
- Subject–verb agreement, tense consistency
- Punctuation: apostrophes, Oxford-comma consistency, em dash vs hyphen use
- Title case consistency for headlines

## Both languages

- **Consistency across frames**: repeated copy (headlines, CTAs, cycle labels) must be identical on every frame. Compare character by character, including accents and line breaks that change meaning.
- No double spaces, stray characters, or orphaned punctuation
- CTA and legal copy present where expected
- Brand name written correctly: "Amazon Music" (not "Amazon music" or "AmazonMusic")

## Music title formatting

Within copy, **album titles go in italics** and **song/track titles go in quotation marks**. This applies to headlines, body copy, supers, captions, scripts, and copy docs.

- ✅ *Un Verano Sin Ti* by Bad Bunny / "Tití Me Preguntó" by Bad Bunny
- ⚠️ Flag: an album in quotes or plain text, a song in italics or without quotes, or an album and a song formatted the same way
- Apply it to every mention, including repeated ones across frames. Format must be consistent across the whole set.
- Quotation marks: use straight or curly quotes consistently throughout the set. Flag mixed styles (" " vs “ ” vs « »).
- Punctuation and quotes: when the copy is in English, commas and periods go inside the closing quote ("Song," not "Song",). In Spanish, they go outside ("Canción", not "Canción,").
- Styling inside the formatting still follows Amazon Music exactly (see amazon-music-styling.md): *un mechón de pelo* stays lowercase, just italicized.
- Artist names get no italics or quotes.

**Where it doesn't apply** (don't flag these):
- UI-style elements that mimic the Amazon Music app (album tiles, now-playing cards, player bars). These follow how the app displays titles, which is plain text.
- Plain-text fields that can't render italics (e.g., a pasted Instagram or TikTok caption). Mention it once under "Couldn't verify" instead of flagging each one.

To check italics, you have to see the formatting: read the asset image, or the Doc/Slides with formatting. If you only have plain pasted text and can't tell whether something is italic, mark it ⚠️ "formatting not verifiable" rather than passing it.
