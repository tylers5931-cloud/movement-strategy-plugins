# Copy & Grammar Checklist (Spanish + English)

Check every line of transcribed copy. Album, artist, and track names follow Amazon Music styling (see amazon-music-styling.md), not general grammar rules, so don't "correct" stylized names.

## Spanish

- **Accents (tildes)**: álbum, canción, emoción, pulmón, música, también, más (more) vs mas, tú (you) vs tu (your), él vs el, sí vs si, qué/cómo/cuándo/dónde in questions and exclamations, aquí, día, corazón, mamá
- **Opening punctuation**: questions need ¿...? and exclamations need ¡...!
- **Ñ**: año, mañana, sueño (never "ano" for "año")
- **Agreement**: gender/number between articles, nouns, and adjectives (todas las emociones, el álbum); subject–verb agreement
- **Verb forms and tense**: consistent person across a series (e.g., all first-person present: Escucho / Empiezo / Canto / Llego)
- **Capitalization**: Spanish doesn't capitalize days, months, languages, or nationalities mid-sentence; titles in sentence case unless it's a stylized name
- **Common mistakes**: haber/a ver, hay/ahí/ay, echo/hecho, porque/por qué/porqué, a/ha, halla/haya, sino/si no
- **Regional tone**: flag (as a note, not an error) slang that may not travel across markets (e.g., Mexico-only or Argentina-only words, voseo) if the asset is pan-regional

## English

- Spelling, homophones (its/it's, your/you're, their/there)
- Subject–verb agreement, tense consistency
- Punctuation: apostrophes, Oxford-comma consistency, em dash vs hyphen use
- Title case consistency for headlines

## Both languages

- **Consistency across frames**: repeated copy (headlines, CTAs, cycle labels) must be identical on every frame. Compare character by character, including accents and line breaks that change meaning.
- No double spaces, stray characters, or orphaned punctuation
- CTA and legal copy present where expected
- Brand name written correctly: "Amazon Music" (not "Amazon music" or "AmazonMusic")
